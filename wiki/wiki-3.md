# Myths Aroud the World 

Almost every culture, regardless of hisotrical background and duration, has some kind of myths developed. 

China: 

Chinese mythology, in broad strokes:

**Creation**

- **Pangu** — the cosmic giant who split chaos into earth and sky, then became the world: his breath the wind, his eyes the sun and moon, his blood the rivers.
- **Nüwa** — serpent-bodied goddess who shaped the first humans from yellow clay, then patched a broken sky with five-colored stones after a pillar of heaven collapsed.

**The divine bureaucracy**

Chinese myth is unusual in that heaven is run like an imperial civil service. The **Jade Emperor** presides over a court of ministers, clerks, and regional officials. Even the **Kitchen God** files an annual report on each household's conduct.

**The Eight Immortals**

A band of transcendents, each with a magic implement — Li Tieguai with his gourd, Lan Caihe of ambiguous gender, He Xiangu the lone woman. They cross the sea by their own means rather than sharing a boat, which became a proverb for individual talent.

**Journey to the West**

Sixteenth-century novel that absorbed centuries of folk material. **Sun Wukong**, the Monkey King, is born from a stone, learns immortality, wrecks heaven's peach banquet, and is pinned under a mountain for five hundred years before escorting the monk Xuanzang to India. He's arguably the most beloved figure in East Asian literature.

**Recurring figures**

- **Dragons** — benevolent water-and-rain deities, not the treasure-hoarding monsters of European myth
- **Chang'e** — drank the elixir of immortality and floated to the moon, where she lives with a jade rabbit
- **The Cowherd and Weaver Girl** — separated lovers who meet once a year on a bridge of magpies (the Qixi festival)
- **Yu the Great** — tamed the world-flood by dredging channels rather than damming, and founded the Xia dynasty

The tradition has no single scripture. It's a braid of Daoist cosmology, Buddhist imports, Confucian ancestor-reverence, and regional folk religion — often contradicting itself, which nobody minded.

Europe:

European mythology isn't one tradition — it's several that barely talk to each other. The main branches:

**Greek / Roman**

The best-documented. Chaos → Gaia → Titans → Olympians, each overthrow more violent than the last. Zeus rules by force rather than mandate. What distinguishes it: the gods are *petty* — vain, adulterous, spiteful — and humans suffer mostly for being noticed by them. Prometheus stealing fire, Persephone and the pomegranate, the Trojan War, Odysseus taking ten years to get home.

**Norse**

The world is made from the corpse of the giant Ymir; Yggdrasil the world-tree connects nine realms. Odin hangs himself on that tree for nine nights to win the runes, and trades an eye for wisdom. Its defining feature is fatalism: the gods *know* Ragnarök is coming, know who kills whom, and march out anyway. Loki is a genuine problem — blood-brother to Odin, father of the wolf that eats him.

**Celtic**

Fragmentary, since the druids wrote nothing down. Irish material survives best: the Tuatha Dé Danann, the Morrígan as crow-goddess of battle, Cú Chulainn's battle-frenzy that deformed his body. The Otherworld sits alongside ours rather than above it — you can walk into it and return to find a century gone.

**Slavic**

Perun the thunder god versus Veles the serpent of the underworld, a duel replayed every storm. Rich in domestic and wild spirits — the *domovoi* behind the stove, the *rusalka* in the river, Baba Yaga in her house on chicken legs. Much was recorded only after Christianization, so the outlines are blurred.

**Finnish / Baltic**

Linguistically unrelated to the rest. The *Kalevala* gives us Väinämöinen, a hero who fights with songs rather than swords, and the Sampo — a magic mill nobody can quite define.

**The interesting contrast with Chinese myth**

No bureaucracy. European pantheons are family dramas — succession, jealousy, inheritance — not civil services. And Norse aside, they mostly lack an eschatology; Greek myth has no end of the world, just an endless present of gods behaving badly.
