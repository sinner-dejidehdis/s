# Cars — Quick Wiki

## History

**1886** — Karl Benz patents the Motorwagen, generally counted as the first true automobile. His wife Bertha then drives it 106 km unannounced to prove it works, inventing both the road trip and the publicity stunt.

**1908** — Ford's Model T. The moving assembly line (1913) cuts build time from 12 hours to 93 minutes and drops the price low enough that Ford's own workers can buy one.

**1970s** — Oil crises kill the American land yacht and open the US market to Japanese economy cars.

**2008–present** — Tesla's Roadster, then the Model S, make electric cars aspirational rather than penitential.

## Engine types

**Internal combustion (ICE)** — Four strokes: intake, compression, power, exhaust. Petrol engines spark-ignite; diesels compress hard enough to ignite fuel without a spark, which is why they make more torque and less noise per liter.

**Layouts** — Inline-4 (cheap, smooth enough), V6, V8 (the American default), flat/boxer (Porsche and Subaru, low center of gravity), inline-6 (naturally balanced, BMW's signature).

**Hybrid** — Petrol engine plus electric motor. Toyota's Prius (1997) made it mainstream.

**EV** — Battery and motor, no gearbox needed. Instant torque, roughly 90% efficient versus 30% for ICE.

## Drivetrain

- **FWD** — Front-wheel drive. Cheap, packages well, understeers when pushed.
- **RWD** — Rear-wheel drive. Better balance, the enthusiast's preference, worse in snow.
- **AWD/4WD** — All four wheels driven. AWD is automatic and road-biased; 4WD is selectable and built for off-road.

## Body styles

Sedan (three-box, separate trunk) · Hatchback (rear door includes the glass) · Wagon/Estate · SUV · Crossover (SUV shape on a car platform — now the dominant body style worldwide) · Coupé · Convertible · Pickup

## Key concepts

**Horsepower vs torque** — Torque is twisting force; horsepower is torque multiplied by rpm. Torque gets you moving, horsepower keeps you accelerating.

**0–60 mph** — The standard acceleration benchmark. Fast modern EVs do it under 3 seconds, which is quicker than most supercars of 20 years ago.

**Turbocharging** — Uses exhaust gas to force more air into the engine. More power from less displacement, at the cost of some throttle lag.

**Platform sharing** — One underlying architecture spawns many models across brands. VW's MQB underpins everything from a Golf to an Audi Q3.

## Where it's going

Electrification is accelerating but unevenly — Norway is near-total, much of the world is barely started. Autonomy has stalled around Level 2 (hands-on driver assistance) with genuine Level 4 confined to geofenced robotaxi fleets. And nearly every manufacturer is now fighting over software and infotainment, which is a strange thing for the car industry to have become.
