# Sports — Quick Wiki

**Soccer / Football**
Codified in England, 1863. 11 per side, 90 minutes, hands forbidden except the keeper. The world's most-played sport; the World Cup outdraws the Olympics.

**Basketball**
Invented 1891 by James Naismith with a peach basket and a soccer ball, as indoor winter conditioning. 5 per side, four quarters. The 24-second shot clock (1954) saved the pro game from stalling tactics.

**Cricket**
England, 16th century. Two teams of 11, bowler versus batter, runs scored between wickets. Test matches last five days and can still end in a draw — a fact non-followers find genuinely difficult to accept.

**Tennis**
Descended from French *jeu de paume* (palm game). Scoring runs 15-30-40-game; nobody is certain why. Four majors: Australian, French, Wimbledon, US Open.

**Baseball**
American, mid-1800s, from English bat-and-ball games. Nine innings, three outs each. The only major sport with no clock — theoretically infinite.

**American Football**
Split from rugby in the 1870s under Walter Camp, who added the line of scrimmage and downs. Four 15-minute quarters, four downs to gain ten yards.

**Rugby**
Union (15 players) and League (13) split in 1895 over paying players. Forward passes illegal; play continues through contact.

**Ice Hockey**
Canada, 1870s. Six per side, three 20-minute periods. Fastest team sport by puck speed — over 100 mph on a slap shot.

**Athletics / Track and Field**
The oldest organized sport; the ancient Olympics began with a footrace in 776 BC. Sprints, distance, jumps, throws, combined events.

**Swimming**
Four competitive strokes: freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, butterfly. Butterfly emerged in the 1930s as a breaststroke loophole and became its own event in 1952.

**Golf**
Scotland, 15th century. 18 holes, lowest strokes wins. Majors: Masters, PGA, US Open, The Open.

**Boxing**
Ancient, modernized by the Queensberry Rules (1867), which introduced gloves and three-minute rounds. Scored by judges across weight classes.

**Cycling**
Road, track, mountain, BMX. The Tour de France (est. 1903) runs roughly 3,500 km over three weeks.

**Volleyball**
Invented 1895 as a gentler alternative to basketball. Six per side indoors, two on sand. Rally scoring to 25.

**Table Tennis**
Victorian after-dinner parlor game turned Olympic sport. Chinese players have won the overwhelming majority of Olympic golds since 1988.

**Formula 1**
Motor racing since 1950. Ten teams, twenty drivers, ~24 races a year. Cars generate enough downforce to theoretically drive upside down.
